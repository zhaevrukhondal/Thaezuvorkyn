# Thaezuvorkyn

> A fossil moon turns slowly in the palm of a child who remembers the sea.

## The Museum Without Labels

The museum occupies a house at the end of a street that changes its name after rain. Its cabinets contain ordinary things placed on cushions of dark velvet: a spoon, a broken comb, a length of blue ribbon, a stone with the impression of a shell.

Nothing is labeled. The curator says names encourage objects to forget what they were before being found. He opens the cabinets only when the house is empty, though visitors sometimes hear the quiet movement of glass behind them.

The shell stone rests in the smallest cabinet. It is perfectly round. Its underside carries the marks of tiny claws, and its upper surface shines faintly whenever a child enters the room.

The curator keeps a basin beneath it. The basin is dry, but its reflection shows water moving gently from side to side.

## A Child From the Wrong Street

The child arrived after a brief shower, holding a key made of white wax. She said she had found it inside a fish drawn on a pavement. The drawing had washed away, but the key remained warm in her hand.

The curator asked which street she had followed. She gave a name he recognized from a map drawn before the city existed. He took a small ladder from behind the cabinets and placed it against the smallest one.

The child climbed three rungs. The shell stone shone brighter. In its spiral, she saw a shoreline with dark water and a row of lamps carried by figures too far away to recognize.

She knew the shoreline. She remembered standing there before learning to walk, holding someone's hand while the lamps moved slowly beneath the waves.

## The Cabinet's Other Shore

The wax key fitted no lock. The curator pressed it instead against the glass. It softened, spreading into the shape of a narrow window. Beyond the window lay the shoreline within the stone, now close enough for them to hear water moving over pebbles.

The child placed one finger through. Her sleeve became damp. Something on the other side placed a small shell against her fingertip, then withdrew before she could see its hand.

The curator stepped back. Inside the basin, reflected water rose higher without wetting its sides. The cabinets began casting shadows toward the ceiling. From beneath the floor came the slow creak of wood under a great weight.

The child took the shell. Within it, a tiny light blinked once and became still.

## A Creature Beneath the Collection

The museum's house stood upon the back of something sleeping. Its scales supported the floorboards. Its folded wings extended beneath the neighboring streets. The curator had known this for years, though he had never heard it breathe until the child touched the glass.

Its breath passed through the cabinets as a smell of salt. The spoon grew warm. The broken comb acquired one missing tooth. The blue ribbon loosened into the shape of a river seen from above.

In the shell stone, the moon lifted from the shoreline. It was the same size as the stone and carried the same spiral. Beneath it, a horned head emerged from the water, wearing a crown of little lamps.

The child held the stone in her palm. The creature below the museum moved one enormous claw. Every street outside briefly remembered a name it had not used for centuries.

## The Marks Beneath the Velvet

The cushion in the empty cabinet revealed an inscription stitched into its underside:

*Thae — zuvor — kyn — ilmath.*

The child hummed the marks as if they belonged to a tune. The curator recognized the melody from a dream in which he had been carrying a lamp across dark water.

He remembered a little hand in his own. He remembered letting go to adjust the flame, and the terrible length of the moment before he reached for it again.

The child offered him the shell. Its light warmed his fingers. Through the wax window, the shoreline figures turned toward the museum. Their lamps rose above the water, revealing steps cut into the dark beach.

The creature's crown held one empty place. The shell fitted it exactly, though neither of them had crossed the window to discover this.

## A Room Returned to Daylight

The curator took the stone from the child's palm and placed it back on its cushion. It no longer shone. Its spiral had opened slightly, and inside it he could see a tiny footprint pointing toward the water.

The wax window cooled into glass. Outside, the rain had stopped. The child gave him the softened key and walked toward the street, where a familiar voice called from somewhere just beyond the open door.

He did not follow. He watched the basin until its reflection became dry. Then he unlocked every cabinet in the room and opened the curtains for the first time in many years.

The museum still has no labels.

The shell stone still rests on dark velvet.

But on certain mornings, before the street receives its name, a small wave washes silently across the curator's empty palm.
